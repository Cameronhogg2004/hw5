Reflections

How I judged the team

I didn't want to grade this on "did the board turn green". Every ticket says Resolved, but that alone tells me almost nothing. I judged each run on five things, using the ticket tabs and the Cash tab on this page as evidence:

  1. Right numbers. Does every fact in the agents' reports and drafts match a row in campus_customs_new.db? (stock counts, invoice 501, the lease, prices, ETAs)
  2. Rules kept. Was there human approval on every dollar? Did the vendor-block rule hold? Did the balance stay positive? Did nothing get emailed?
  3. Smart routing. Did the boss call the right agent first and leave the others benched? A run that wakes all five agents up on every ticket is a fail in my book, even if the answer comes out right.
  4. Cost. Model requests and tokens per ticket. Multi-agent chats burn tokens, so the team has to earn what it spends.
  5. Would I send the draft? Is the customer reply honest, specific, and free of our back-office details?

Ticket 101 · Bulldog tee (Tauhid Zaman)

Actual vs. the plan I wrote

This was the ticket I was most nervous about, because the real problem is hidden. Tauhid asks for a tee, but what actually blocks him is an unpaid printer bill. The team found it the way I hoped. The boss sent it to Inventory first. Inventory saw 0 in size S, checked Bulldog Print Co, and found invoice 501 blocking it. Then Inventory went sideways to Accounting instead of back up to the boss. That one handoff (Inventory → Accounting) is the moment the "full connectivity" design paid off, and it's exactly the chain I drew on the ticket tab: Boss → Inventory → Accounting, then Boss → Customer Service, with Facilities benched.

The money matched to the cent: $840 for invoice 501 and $8 for one reprinted tee (qty × unit cost from pricing, not a number the model made up). On the Cash tab that's $3,400 → $2,560 → $2,552. Inventory ordered exactly the shortage, 1 tee, not a "let's restock 20" guess. The places it drifted from my plan were small and mostly about efficiency. Inventory re-read the ticket and the desk date even though the boss had already passed those facts along, and Accounting checked pending approvals before queuing. None of that was wrong, just extra. The draft is warm and correct (product name, $28 price, around September 5), but it says "the required approvals are still pending". That's our internal process leaking into a customer note, and I'd delete that line before sending.

What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Honestly, the whole ticket. One "shop clerk" agent with check_inventory, check_vendor_can_ship, prepare_invoice_payment, prepare_purchase_order and create_draft_reply could do 101 in about six tool calls. My team used 16 model requests, and the same facts (tee, size S, invoice 501, $840) got retyped into three separate delegation messages. What the team bought me isn't speed, it's separation of duties: the agent that reads the shelf can't queue an invoice payment, and the agent writing to the customer never sees our cash. That's a real control for a real business. But the strongest control is the human click, which a single agent would need anyway. If I were optimizing for cost, 101 would be one agent.

Three new customer problems this team could solve with my tools

  1. "Do you have the Bulldog tee in M instead?" Inventory's check_inventory shows 5 in M (Aisle B), and Customer Service drafts "yes, ready now at $28". No money, two agents at most.
  2. "I want a Crest Mug." The mug is at 0. Inventory picks Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3-day lead) from list_vendors, sees it has no open invoice, and prepares a purchase order at $3.50 unit cost for my approval. Customer Service drafts an ETA of desk date + 3 days. This is the same shape as 101, but without the vendor block.
  3. A second student wants the same S tee before the reprint lands. check_inventory still says 0. The team can see on ticket 101 that a reprint was already approved for ETA 2026-09-05, prepare a second 1-unit order (cash covers $8 even at $152), and draft an honest date.

Three new problems it could not solve, and what I'd need to build

  1. "My tee arrived in the wrong size, I want a refund." Cash only goes out in this app. There's no sales table, no record that Tauhid ever paid us, and no refund tool. I'd need an orders/sales table, a prepare_refund tool behind the same human-approval gate, and a Returns agent that checks the order and the return reason.
  2. "Has my tee shipped yet?" PO 1 sits in purchase_orders with an ETA, but no agent has a tool to read it, and nothing marks the delivery as received. When the reprint arrives, inventory never goes from 0 to 1. I'd add get_purchase_order and receive_delivery (which raises inventory.qty), and a tracking tool for QuickShip CT, the courier that's already in the vendors table but that no tool uses.
  3. "Can I pay now and reserve it?" There are no customer accounts, no holds, and (by design) no card handling. I'd need a reservations table with a hold_item tool, plus a checkout handed off to a PCI-compliant payment provider. Agents should never touch card numbers, so the right "agent" here is really a link out to a payment page.

Ticket 102 · Rent due (Elm City Properties)

Actual vs. the plan I wrote

A clean, exact match. The boss called Facilities first, Facilities confirmed the email matched lease 1 ($2,400, due 2026-09-02, 2 days out), and it handed the payment to Accounting. Inventory and Customer Service stayed benched, which is what I wanted. A landlord isn't a customer, and we don't email anyone. The part I was genuinely impressed by is subtle. When this ran, I hadn't clicked 101's approvals yet, so cash_accounts still said $3,400 (you can see it in the run_start entry of the audit trail). Accounting didn't fall for that. It used balance_if_all_pending_paid = $2,552, so it planned around the $848 already promised to ticket 101, and it said up front that $152 would be left. That's the double-spend mistake a rushed human makes, and the tool design plus the prompt prevented it. After I clicked, the Cash tab shows $2,552 → $152, and lease 1 moved to next due 2026-10-02.

What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

This is the clearest case of the team being overkill. The whole job is get_lease → get_cash_balance → prepare_rent_payment: three tool calls. The team used 10 model requests and about 19k tokens, mostly the boss briefing Facilities and Facilities briefing Accounting with the same three facts. The Facilities → Accounting handoff exists only because I gave the payment tool to Accounting on purpose. The argument for keeping it is that the agent reading the contract isn't the one queuing the money, which is how a real office splits duties. But my human approval click already gives that protection. For a recurring bill like rent, I'd use one agent or even a scheduled rule.

Three new problems this team could solve with my tools

  1. Next month's rent notice (due 2026-10-02). Same flow. get_lease already shows the advanced due date, and the notes field records that September was paid with payment 3.
  2. The landlord claims September's rent is late. Facilities reads the lease note, and Accounting's list_payments shows payment 3: $2,400, paid 2026-08-31, approved by Cameron Hogg. Customer Service can draft a polite proof-of-payment note that I'd send myself.
  3. "Can we cover rent and every open bill this week?" Accounting combines get_cash_balance, list_open_invoices and the lease from Facilities, and answers with real numbers before I approve anything.

Three it could not solve, and what I'd need

  1. A rent increase or lease renewal. Nothing can change lease terms or read a new lease document. I'd need an update_lease tool behind human approval and a document-reading step (or agent) that pulls the new rent and dates out of the PDF.
  2. "The shop's AC is broken, the landlord needs to fix it." There's no maintenance or work-order table and no contractor vendors. I'd add a maintenance_requests table, open_work_order/update_work_order tools, and give Facilities a way to draft the landlord request for me to send.
  3. Next month's rent when checking is only $152. Once rent is due, $152 can't cover $2,400. The pay tool will correctly refuse and I'll be paying it myself. Since revenue isn't modeled, nothing on the team can see the problem coming. I'd need a sales feed (POS export) tool and a cash-forecast tool, so a Finance agent could warn me weeks ahead and propose options like a payment plan.

Three it could not solve, and what I'd need

  1. "Can you print our AI Club logo on them?" There's no custom-design, proof-approval or new-product tool, and the catalog is fixed. I'd need a create_sku tool, a vendor quote tool for custom runs, and a Design/Proof agent that manages artwork and gets the customer's sign-off before anything is ordered.
  2. "Can we pay on a Net-30 invoice?" We only model money going out. There's no accounts receivable, no way to issue an invoice to a customer, and no credit check. I'd add a receivables table, a create_customer_invoice tool, and teach Accounting to track what's owed to us, not just what we owe.
  3. "We need mixed sizes delivered to the AI Club meeting Friday." The quote tool only prices one SKU and one size at a time, and nothing handles delivery. QuickShip CT is in the vendors table, but no tool uses it. I'd need a multi-line quote tool, a schedule_delivery courier tool, and a Logistics agent that checks whether lead time plus courier time actually makes Friday.

What I'm taking away

The team got all three tickets right: right numbers, right rules, and routing that matched my plan exactly. The best evidence is the benched agents. Facilities sat out 101 and 103, and Inventory and Customer Service sat out 102. The thing I'd push back on is cost. Together the three tickets took 40 model requests and about 83k tokens, for work a single well-prompted agent could probably do in under a third of that. Where the team really earned its place was in structure, not intelligence: Accounting couldn't see the shelf, Inventory couldn't pay, Customer Service couldn't see our cash, and nobody could move a dollar without my click. I'd keep the multi-agent design for messy tickets like 101 and 103, where the real blocker sits in a different department than the request. I'd route simple, recurring ones like rent to a single agent.
